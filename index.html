01 / About

From a scientific question
to a clinical tool.

I study how the brain works—and how better measurements can help us understand it.

I earned my PhD in Biomedical Engineering at Boston University in May 2025 and am completing my MD, expected in May 2027. My research has focused on cerebral blood flow, optical sensing, and the relationship between neural activity and hemodynamics.

That work has taken me from optical systems and quantitative analysis to neurocritical care studies. Alongside research and clinical training, I’ve co-founded a sleep apnea screening startup and explored how biomedical technologies can move toward clinical use.

Optical tools for cerebral blood flow monitoring

In collaboration with Meta’s Reality Labs, I engineered a fiber-based speckle contrast optical spectroscopy (SCOS) system for continuous human cerebral blood flow monitoring.

  • Led a cross-functional study in a neurocritical care unit to investigate correlates of coma recovery using compact blood flow monitors.
  • Developed an end-to-end analysis pipeline for blood flow index estimation, improving the accuracy of speckle contrast estimates.
